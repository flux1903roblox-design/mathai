# Structured-practice and AI-tutor math apps for middle school (grades 6-9): how each designs a lesson on a phone (state as of 2025-2026)

Scope: Khan Academy and Khanmigo, Brilliant (including its Koji AI tutor), IXL Math, Zearn, Smartick, Matific, DreamBox (Discovery Education), and AI tutors from 2024-2026 (ChatGPT Study Mode, Gemini Guided Learning, Google LearnLM on Eedi, Snorkl). Carnegie Learning MATHia is added as a middle-school reference point. Research date: 2026-11-06.

Source-quality note: Common Sense Education stopped publishing and updating edtech reviews in January 2026, and said its review pages would be removed in February 2026, so its reviews of these apps can no longer be checked ([Common Sense Education FAQ, reached via the Zearn review URL](https://www.commonsense.org/education/reviews/zearn)). Several vendor help centers (Zearn, DreamBox Zendesk) returned HTTP 403 to direct fetches. Findings from those pages come from search-result excerpts of the official pages and are marked "(via search excerpt)".

## 1. Who each app is for, platforms, price, scale and reputation, and independent efficacy evidence

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced tools are the structured-practice platforms: Zearn and DreamBox are rated "Strong" by Evidence for ESSA, IXL has one small RCT, and Khan Academy plus Khanmigo has a 2026 two-year cluster RCT in middle schools. Their effect sizes are small (about +0.06 to +0.14 SD), and most of the strongest studies were done in elementary grades, not grades 7-8. The AI-tutor evidence (Khanmigo RCT, Bastani/PNAS, Google-Eedi) says a guardrailed tutor does no harm and can help, but students rarely engage it unless the design pushes them to.

### Cited Findings

**Khan Academy and Khanmigo**
- The iOS app is rated 4.6 from about 113K ratings and is an Editors' Choice. It covers math and other subjects in English plus 25 more languages and supports offline downloads. "Not all functionality is available in this app": teacher and parent tools, test prep and community features are web-only. — [App Store: Khan Academy](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/khan-academy/id469863705)
- Khanmigo consumer pricing is reported at about $4/month or $44/year. US teachers get the teacher tools free through a Microsoft partnership. District pricing is not published, and third-party figures conflict ($2-4 per student per month). — [Neowin (free for US educators)](https://www.neowin.net/news/khan-academys-khanmigo-ai-tool-to-be-free-for-us-educators-thanks-to-microsoft/); [aitoolsatlas pricing (secondary)](https://aitoolsatlas.ai/tools/khanmigo/pricing)
- Scale: Khanmigo access grew from about 40,000 students in 2023 to nearly 1 million in 2026, but regular use stagnated. Sal Khan called its reception a "non-event" for many students (Chalkbeat, April 2026, via summaries). — [Let's Data Science summary of Chalkbeat](https://letsdatascience.com/news/ai-tutors-fail-to-spark-widespread-learning-gains-3efac9c8); [WFTV/AP](https://www.wftv.com/news/khan-academyrsquos/2UFWOVJK2AZLRJFD5YZVO36IPQ/)
- **Main RCT (Oreopoulos & Low, EdWorkingPaper 26-1551, August 2026).** Two-year cluster RCT in 18 Tennessee (Hamilton County) middle schools, inside daily remedial (RTI) math sessions for grades 6-8. Khanmigo was configured to coach rather than give answers. Assignment raised math achievement by 1.3 national percentile ranks per term, about 0.06-0.08 SD over a school year. The implied effect of a full year of active participation is 0.14 SD. The primary sample was 6,902 student-terms, and treated students averaged about 30 minutes of practice per week. The gains "resemble those from Khan Academy practice without AI assistance". The authors attribute them "primarily to structured, individually targeted practice rather than to conversational tutoring." The study was pre-registered (AEARCTR-0013519). — [EdWorkingPaper 26-1551 (PDF)](https://edworkingpapers.com/sites/default/files/ai26-1551.pdf)
- I found no Evidence for ESSA rating for Khan Academy, only an uncited claim on a third-party blog. — [Branching Minds blog](https://www.branchingminds.com/blog/math-reading-interventions)

**IXL Math**
- Evidence for ESSA lists one Johns Hopkins cluster RCT: grades 3-5, four elementary schools in Holland, Michigan, spring 2023, 545 students. Effect was +0.13 on Renaissance Star Math and +0.03 (not significant) on the state M-STEP test, for an average of +0.08. IXL markets this as ESSA Tier 1. — [Evidence for ESSA program page](https://evidenceforessa.org/?p=225); [IXL ESSA page](https://www.ixl.com/ESSA)
- SRI reviewed an IXL-commissioned quasi-experimental study (179 Oklahoma schools using IXL in grades 3-8 against 179 comparison schools, three years) and judged it "moderate" (ESSA Tier 2) evidence. — [SRI publication](https://www.sri.com/publication/education-learning-pubs/early-learning-pubs/ixl-math-nonregulatory-essa-standards-evidence-review-what-works-clearinghouse-standards-review/)
- Ratings: app stores show about 4 stars, while Trustpilot-type sites show about 1.1-2. The likely reason is that review sites attract self-selected, negative reviewers. — [MonsterMath blog (secondary, competitor)](https://www.monstermath.app/blog/ixl-reviews-from-real-users-should-you-use-it-in-2026)

**Zearn**
- Free: "daily digital lessons and teaching materials are available in Zearn accounts—all for free, always." The middle school product covers grades 6-8 and "every objective of middle school math." — [Zearn middle school page](https://about.zearn.org/middle-school-math)
- Scale (company claim): used by "1 in 4 elementary-school students" and more than 1 million middle-school students. As of February 2025, over 60% of partners were Title I schools. A K-8 curriculum followed the K-5 one in 2022. — [Wikipedia: Zearn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zearn); [Zearn MS page](https://about.zearn.org/middle-school-math)
- Evidence for ESSA rated Zearn "Strong" in 2025, with an average effect size of +0.09 across 3 studies. Wikipedia also cites a RAND RCT with significant gains on NWEA MAP. — [Wikipedia: Zearn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zearn)
- Zearn's middle school content follows the Illustrative Mathematics scope and sequence and aligns with Eureka Math/EngageNY. — [Zearn help (via search excerpt)](https://help.zearn.org/hc/en-us/articles/4403432495127-Can-Zearn-Math-for-Middle-School-be-used-Alongside-Other-high-Quality-Instructional-Materials)

**DreamBox Math (Discovery Education)**
- DreamBox is rated "Strong" by Evidence for ESSA. The rating rests on an SRI study in three Rocketship charter schools with over 500 **K-1** students, where DreamBox users outscored controls on NWEA MAP. The rating page shows no grades 6-8 evidence, and the PDF filename suggests the study dates from about 2011. — [Discovery Education: DreamBox rated Strong](https://www.discoveryeducation.com/resources/research/dreambox-math-rated-strong-by-evidence-for-essa/)
- Vendor claim: students who complete at least five lessons a week can expect "an average of 1.5 grade levels of growth by the end of the school year." This comes from the 2022 grades 6-8 implementation guide, and the guide is older than the research window. — [DreamBox 6-8 Implementation Success Guide (PDF, ©2022)](https://info.discoveryeducation.com/rs/063-SDC-839/images/TeacherGuide-6-8-Math.pdf)

**Brilliant**
- Founded 2012 (Sue Khim), about 10 million users in 2025, freemium. Wikipedia states the monthly price as US$45 (October 2026) with an annual discount. Third-party reviews cite about $24.99-27.99 monthly or $13.49/month billed annually. The figures conflict, and Brilliant says prices vary by region, platform and promotion. — [Wikipedia: Brilliant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brilliant_(website)); [Brighterly comparison](https://brighterly.com/blog/brilliant-vs-khan-academy/); [Brilliant pricing help](https://brilliant.org/help/pricing-and-plans)
- Free tier: "two per day" lessons, sequential course navigation, ads, and a "limited preview" of the Koji AI tutor. Premium adds unlimited lessons and practice, jumping to any lesson, and full Koji access. The math curriculum "begins with grade 4 foundations" and runs through calculus (page updated 29 Sept 2026). — [Brilliant: Is Brilliant worth it?](https://brilliant.org/resources/choosing-brilliant/is-brilliant-worth-it/)
- iOS: rated 4.7 from 33K ratings, age rating 4+. The listing positions the app from about grade 5 through college and sells "Premium with Tutor" tiers. — [App Store: Brilliant](https://apps.apple.com/app/brilliant-learn-by-doing/id913335252)
- Efficacy: only Brilliant's own July 2026 behavioral analysis of middle- and high-school learners who asked Koji for help. 94% made another attempt, 66% later submitted a correct answer to the same problem without Koji revealing it, and 87% of those who moved on answered the next problem correctly. Brilliant itself calls these "behavioral signals, not proof." — [Brilliant: Is Brilliant worth it?](https://brilliant.org/resources/choosing-brilliant/is-brilliant-worth-it/)
- Brilliant partners with the MATHCOUNTS Foundation for middle school math. — [Tech & Learning](https://www.techlearning.com/learning/stem/what-is-brilliant-and-how-can-teachers-use-it)

**Smartick**
- Daily adaptive math for ages 4-14. App Store content is split into ages 4-5, 6-8, 9-12 and 13-14. Subscriptions are per child. — [App Store: Smartick](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/smartick/id823586852); [Trustpilot](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/smartick.com)
- Price is unclear. Figures range from about $24-49/month per child, with discounts for annual plans, and sources conflict. — [Smarter Learning Guide review](https://smarterlearningguide.com/smartick-math-review); [Subger (weak source)](https://subger.com/en/service/smartick)
- Trustpilot score is 4.5 from about 2K reviews. Efficacy figures are company-only: "92% of students improve their calculation skills, and 83% improve their school grade in Math." I found no independent study. — [Trustpilot](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/smartick.com); [Analytics Insight listing](https://www.analyticsinsight.net/specifications/smartick)

**Matific**
- K-6 platform ("2000+ activities"), so it is largely below the grades 7-8 target. — [MindShare Learning review](https://mindsharelearning.ca/?p=13582)
- Evidence: an SEG Measurement study (2016-17, grades 2-3, Hampton City, Virginia) found effect size .19 overall. Attard (2016, grades 1-6) reported an average 34% test gain across eight schools. A 2026 vendor press release (Smith Family, Australia) reports "Grade 6: +5.0%", which is not a controlled study. — [Heinemann Matific page](https://heinemann.com/matific); [AAP press release May 2026](https://www.aap.com.au/aapreleases/cision20260507ae54706/)

**Carnegie Learning MATHia (added reference point)**
- The blended core curriculum meets ESSA Tier 1 "Strong" based on a RAND study funded by the US Department of Education (company figure: 18,000+ students, 147 schools, 7 states). MATHia used alone meets Tier 2 "Moderate." Both claims come from company materials. — [Carnegie Learning research page](https://www.carnegielearning.com/research/); [Carnegie Learning MS solution](https://www.carnegielearning.com/solutions/math/middle-school-math-solution)

**General-purpose AI tutors (2025)**
- OpenAI launched ChatGPT Study Mode on 29 July 2025, and Google launched Gemini Guided Learning on 6 August 2025. Neither has published controlled learning-outcome studies. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/06/google-takes-on-chatgpts-study-mode-with-new-guided-learning-tool-in-gemini/)

### Inferences
- For a grades 7-8 product, the evidence supports **structured, targeted practice** as the main driver of gains. The Khanmigo RCT shows that adding an AI chat did not raise the effect above plain Khan Academy practice.
- "Strong" ESSA badges (DreamBox, partly IXL) often rest on elementary samples. They should not be taken as evidence for middle school.
- Realistic effect sizes for this kind of app are about +0.05 to +0.15 SD per year at roughly 30-45 minutes of practice a week. That is a useful benchmark for any claim the team makes.

### Gaps
- I could not open the Evidence for ESSA pages for Zearn or DreamBox directly. Zearn's +0.09 figure comes via Wikipedia, and I could not confirm which grades its three studies covered.
- I found no reliable current IXL family price or Khan Academy district price.
- A "Stanford study (June 2026)" said students in two districts used AI tutors only a few minutes per week. I saw it only in a secondary summary with inconsistent numbers, and it is unverified ([Let's Data Science](https://letsdatascience.com/news/ai-tutors-fail-to-spark-widespread-learning-gains-3efac9c8)).
- One search summary mentioned a Common Sense Media report (August 2025) on Khanmigo amplifying gender stereotypes. I did not retrieve the primary source.

## 2. Lesson structure: length, number of problems, how mastery is defined, and adaptive difficulty

### Takeaway
The apps fall into two models. The first is **skill-score mastery**: IXL's 0-100 SmartScore and Khan's per-skill levels with 100 mastery points. The second is a **fixed daily lesson with gated completion**: Zearn's warm-up, guided practice and Tower of Power, DreamBox's 10-15 minute adaptive lessons at five a week, and Smartick's 15-minute daily session. The fixed lesson fits a phone and a habit loop better. The skill score is clearer for teachers but produces frustration when a single wrong answer drops the score.

### Cited Findings

**IXL SmartScore (official guide)**
- Scored 0-100. It is "not a percent-correct grade" and moves up and down with correctness, question difficulty, recent answers and consistency. — [IXL SmartScore Guide (PDF)](https://www.ixl.com/materials/SmartScore_Guide.pdf)
- From 0 to 70, correct answers bring large increases and wrong answers small penalties. From 71 to 90, questions run from mid- to high-level rigor. 80 counts as "proficient" and "could be the perfect stopping point." — [IXL SmartScore Guide](https://www.ixl.com/materials/SmartScore_Guide.pdf)
- At 90 the student enters the **Challenge Zone**. There, a correct answer adds only **1-2 points** and a wrong answer costs **3-8 points**, and students "need to answer as many as 10 questions correctly in a row" to reach 100. — [IXL SmartScore Guide](https://www.ixl.com/materials/SmartScore_Guide.pdf)
- For most skills, students must complete at least 28 questions to reach 100. IXL recommends reaching 80 in at least two skills per week. The algorithm is proprietary. — [IXL SmartScore guide (analytics PDF, via search excerpt)](https://www.ixl.com/analytics/smart-score-guide.pdf); [IXL SmartScore Guide](https://www.ixl.com/materials/SmartScore_Guide.pdf)

**Khan Academy mastery**
- Each skill is worth 100 mastery points: Familiar = 50, Proficient = 80, Mastered = 100. A student reaches Familiar by scoring about 70-85% on an exercise or by getting every question on the skill right in a quiz, test or challenge. Proficient requires all correct on the skill in an exercise, quiz, unit test or course challenge. Mastered requires already being Proficient and then getting all correct on a Unit Test or Course Challenge. A mastered skill can drop to Familiar on a score below 70%. — [Khan Academy Help: How do mastery levels work?](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/bg/articles/5548760867853); [Khan Help: Course and Unit Mastery](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/115002552631)
- Course Mastery is the share of skills at Proficient or Mastered. — [Khan Help: Course and Unit Mastery](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/articles/115002552631)
- In the Tennessee RCT, practice was placed automatically from each student's NWEA MAP scores into personalized learning paths across the four MAP middle-school strands, and teachers could adjust placements. Mature implementation reached 38-45 minutes a week with about 3.7 skills brought to proficiency. — [EdWorkingPaper 26-1551](https://edworkingpapers.com/sites/default/files/ai26-1551.pdf)
- FutureEd (Sept 2026) mentions "the five correct answers in a row needed to advance" and a plan to count a bot-assisted redo toward that streak. — [FutureEd](https://www.future-ed.org/students-tried-khanmigos-ai-tutor-most-didnt-want-its-help/)

**Zearn digital lesson (one lesson per day, about 30 minutes)**
- Flow: fluency warm-up, then Guided Practice, then **Tower of Power**, then a paper Exit Ticket. — [Zearn help: Understanding digital lessons (via search excerpt)](https://help.zearn.org/hc/en-us/sections/22135502261015-Understanding-Zearn-digital-lessons); [Zearn help (via search excerpt)](https://help.zearn.org/hc/en-us/articles/236238988)
- Guided Practice delivers new concepts with "real on-screen teachers, interactive digital manipulatives, and pause points." It typically has 3-4 problems, one of which usually uses the paper Student Notes. — [Zearn G2 Tutoring guide (PDF, via search excerpt)](https://webassets.zearn.org/Implementation/G2_Math_Tutoring_with_Zearn.pdf)
- Tower of Power has "two to four stages of problems that increase in complexity and decrease in scaffolding." Students must complete 100% of it before moving to the next lesson. — [Zearn help: Tower of Power (via search excerpt)](https://help.zearn.org/hc/en-us/articles/236239188)
- Lesson time is about 30 minutes, and sources vary. — [Zearn scheduling guide (via search excerpt)](https://app.alludolearning.com/m/activities/152090-zearn-creating-a-math-schedule)
- One district's grades 6-8 routine is an instructional video, then the Zearn digital lesson, then a printed practice page. — [JCS district Zearn 6-8 guide](https://sites.google.com/jcs-inc.org/jcsmathresources/educational-facilitator-resources/zearn-6-8-for-efs)

**DreamBox grades 6-8 (2022 guide, older than the research window)**
- The recommendation is at least five lessons a week, which is 60-90 min/week at **10-15 min per lesson**. Time varies because of in-lesson adaptivity. — [DreamBox 6-8 guide](https://info.discoveryeducation.com/rs/063-SDC-839/images/TeacherGuide-6-8-Math.pdf)
- There is no placement test ("continuous assessment"). Adaptation happens "both within lessons to provide scaffolding and between lessons to personalize the lesson pathway." Teachers can set the initial placement level only before the student's first login. — [DreamBox 6-8 guide](https://info.discoveryeducation.com/rs/063-SDC-839/images/TeacherGuide-6-8-Math.pdf)
- The system adjusts "difficulty, scaffolding, sequencing, number of hints, and pacing." — [DreamBox help: How does DreamBox Math work? (via search excerpt)](https://dreamboxlearning.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/27281843188243)

**Smartick**
- About 15 minutes a day of adaptive exercises. The session speeds up when the child is learning and slows down when they struggle. Games unlock after the daily session. — [Getting Smart](https://www.gettingsmart.com/2016/11/smart-review-daily-math-app-simple-approach-proven-results/) (2016, older); [App Store: Smartick](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/smartick/id823586852)

**Brilliant**
- Courses are sequences of lessons grouped by concept. Lessons are "hands-on": the learner solves problems, sees visual explanations and builds understanding "step-by-step". Marketing says "as little as 15 minutes a day." — [Brilliant help: Getting started](https://brilliant.org/help/using-brilliant/how-do-i-get-started-on-brilliant/); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/brilliant-learn-by-doing/id913335252)

### Inferences
- A good phone design for grades 7-8 could pair a Zearn/DreamBox-style **fixed 10-15 minute daily lesson** (warm-up, short guided example, then a 2-4 stage gated practice "tower" of increasing difficulty) with a **per-skill score** for the teacher dashboard. The score should be shown to the student as progress, not as a number that falls sharply.
- IXL's Challenge Zone (+1-2 / -3-8, up to 10 in a row) is the clearest example of mastery-gating that causes frustration (see section 6). A gentler rule, such as Khan's "all correct in a set" or Zearn's "finish the tower with Boosts," avoids the punishing feel of a falling score.
- Khan's decay (Mastered can drop to Familiar) is a cheap way to drive spaced review, but the drop needs to be communicated gently.

### Gaps
- I found no official count of problems per Khan exercise set in 2025-2026. Help-center text was only reachable in excerpts, and the FutureEd "five in a row" may describe a new 2026 rule.
- I found no 2024-2026 documentation of DreamBox 6-8 lesson length that updates the 2022 guide.
- I found no official description of a Brilliant lesson's typical screen or problem count.

## 3. Question types and input methods on a phone

### Takeaway
Concept-first apps (Brilliant, DreamBox, Zearn) lean on **interactive manipulatives and visual models**. Practice-first apps (IXL, Khan, Smartick) lean on **typed numeric or expression input through a custom math keypad**, plus multiple choice. Only the newer AI products (Brilliant's Koji, Snorkl) use drawing or handwriting and voice as input. None of the big practice platforms grades free-form handwritten work on a phone.

### Cited Findings
- **Khan Academy:** the mobile keypad uses Khan's open-source `math-input` component (now part of the Perseus exercise framework). It is built "to power math input and expression editing on small screens" and has multiple keypad configurations for fractions and algebraic expressions. — [GitHub: Khan/math-input](https://github.com/Khan/math-input)
- **Khan Academy:** exercises, quizzes and tests give "instant feedback and step-by-step hints." — [App Store: Khan Academy](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/khan-academy/id469863705)
- **Khanmigo:** in the RCT, the tutor was a text chat window. Students could also reach it through suggested prompt buttons beside exercises, for example "Help me solve this. Ask me one question at a time." and "Help me check my answer." — [EdWorkingPaper 26-1551](https://edworkingpapers.com/sites/default/files/ai26-1551.pdf)
- **Brilliant:** learners "manipulate models, trace code, test patterns, and solve problems rather than only watching explanations." Koji "can see the lesson and problem a learner is working on," and the App Store says the tutor can "draw on your screen, highlight parts of a problem, and create visualizations." — [Brilliant: Is it worth it?](https://brilliant.org/resources/choosing-brilliant/is-brilliant-worth-it/); [App Store: Brilliant](https://apps.apple.com/app/brilliant-learn-by-doing/id913335252)
- **Brilliant:** answers go through interactive input fields and multiple choice. One aggregated review criticizes interactivity as "limited to sliders." — [Brighterly: Is Brilliant worth it](https://brighterly.com/blog/is-brilliant-org-worth-it)
- **DreamBox 6-8:** lessons are designed so the learner "problem-solves with digital tools and manipulatives." Students also have a Help button with direction audio. — [DreamBox 6-8 guide](https://info.discoveryeducation.com/rs/063-SDC-839/images/TeacherGuide-6-8-Math.pdf)
- **Zearn MS:** visual models and manipulatives for each concept, problem solving with interactive models, and students may choose their own strategy. — [Zearn MS page](https://about.zearn.org/middle-school-math)
- **IXL:** one review reports a student marked wrong on an answer equivalent to the correct one, which points to input or answer-matching problems. — [Trustpilot IXL reviews](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/ixl.com?page=10)
- **Snorkl (AI feedback, used in 10,000+ schools by a directory's count):** students solve on a digital whiteboard while explaining aloud. The AI analyzes both the audio and the drawing, judging correctness and the strength of the explanation and giving timestamped feedback. Teachers can let feedback show automatically or require their approval first. It supports 40+ languages. — [Snorkl help: What is Snorkl](https://help.snorkl.app/en/articles/12615061-what-is-snorkl); [Ditch That Textbook](https://ditchthattextbook.com/snorkl/); [Tool Directory (scale claim)](https://tooldirectory.ai/tools/snorkl)

### Inferences
- For Hebrew on a phone, a **custom math keypad** in the Khan/Perseus style (digits, fraction, exponent, variable, minus/negative, equals) avoids the system keyboard and RTL/LTR mixing problems. Equivalence checking (CAS-style) is needed so equivalent answers are not marked wrong, which was a recurring IXL complaint.
- Snorkl's whiteboard-plus-voice model and Koji's "sees the lesson state" model are the closest analogues to the planned photo exam checking. Both put the AI on the student's actual work, not on a blank chat box.

### Gaps
- I found no official 2025-2026 documentation of IXL's or Zearn's phone input methods (keypad layout, graphing tools) or of whether their grades 6-8 graphing items work on small phone screens.
- I found no evidence on handwriting or photo input accuracy for any of these platforms.

## 4. Wrong-answer handling: retries, hints, worked solutions, and how AI tutors guide without giving the answer

### Takeaway
Every platform separates **(a) an immediate explanation or scaffold after an error** from **(b) escalation when errors repeat**. Escalation takes the form of an IXL in-skill recommendation, a Zearn Boost and Tower Alert, or a DreamBox limited hint ladder. For AI tutors, the 2024-2026 evidence is consistent. Unguarded AI used as a crutch harms exam performance (Bastani, PNAS 2025). Guardrailed Socratic tutors do no harm, and with human supervision can match human tutors (Google-Eedi). The Khanmigo RCT shows the binding constraint is engagement: most students send bare answers or ignore the tutor. Khan's 2026 response was to make the tutor **appear automatically after a wrong answer**.

### Cited Findings

**IXL**
- After a miss, students are encouraged to "read the explanation to get the key steps and strategies they need to succeed on the next question." If they are still struggling, they "work through an in-skill recommendation" to build prerequisite knowledge. — [IXL SmartScore Guide](https://www.ixl.com/materials/SmartScore_Guide.pdf)

**Zearn**
- **Boosts** are embedded remediation in the Tower of Power. They are scaffolding from prior grades or units, "personalized to the specific struggle." Three Boosts in the same Tower generate a **Tower Alert** to the teacher. — [Zearn help: Tower Alerts Report (via search excerpt)](https://help.zearn.org/hc/en-us/articles/236239748); [Zearn help: Tower of Power (via search excerpt)](https://help.zearn.org/hc/en-us/articles/236239188)

**DreamBox**
- Students can open a limited number of new hints per problem. When no new hints remain, the last one repeats. Hints correspond to the part of the problem to complete first. DreamBox says hints "support thinking without giving away answers." — [DreamBox help: Student experience 3-5 and 6-8 (via search excerpt)](https://dreamboxlearning.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/27281571910931)
- The 6-8 toolbar has a Hint button for "scaffolded hints" and a messaging feature that lets a student "send a lesson screenshot to the teacher dashboard." Teachers are told to coach with prompts ("What did the directions say?… What have you tried?"). — [DreamBox 6-8 guide](https://info.discoveryeducation.com/rs/063-SDC-839/images/TeacherGuide-6-8-Math.pdf)

**Khan Academy and Khanmigo**
- Khanmigo is configured for guided help: "it does not supply answers, instead responding with hints, questions, and step-by-step prompts tied to the student's current problem." — [EdWorkingPaper 26-1551](https://edworkingpapers.com/sites/default/files/ai26-1551.pdf)
- Example exchange from the paper (paraphrased by the authors). K: "I see you wrote 48. Can you tell me how you got that?" S: "6 x 8". K: "Great start. You found the area of a rectangle. For triangles, do you remember what you do next?" For "idk", the reply is "No worries. Look at the short hand. What number is it pointing…" — [EdWorkingPaper 26-1551, Table 4](https://edworkingpapers.com/sites/default/files/ai26-1551.pdf)
- **Engagement data** (563 Year-2 students, 100,017 exercise sessions):
  - 96% of students tried Khanmigo at least once.
  - The median student messaged it on 33% of practice days, in 14% of exercise sessions and in **17% of mistake sessions**. The median number of messages per mistake session was zero.
  - Messages broke down as: **39.4% bare answers** (numbers or short phrases without explanation), 24.2% clicks on suggested prompts, 12.6% low-effort messages or requests for the answer, **14.5% a math question or reasoning step**, and 9.3% off-task (essays, raps, jokes).

  — [EdWorkingPaper 26-1551](https://edworkingpapers.com/sites/default/files/ai26-1551.pdf)
- **UI history:** Khanmigo started as a chat icon plus a "Tutor Me" control at the lower right next to the next-question buttons. On 29 January 2026 Khan switched it to **open by default** shortly after page load. A broader redesign that activates Khanmigo throughout practice followed. — [EdWorkingPaper 26-1551](https://edworkingpapers.com/sites/default/files/ai26-1551.pdf)
- **2026 redesign** (FutureEd, 30 Sept 2026):
  - Khanmigo is built into practice rather than a separate tab.
  - Before an attempt, it offers little beyond a hint and encouragement.
  - **After a wrong answer it pops up automatically** to work through the problem.
  - Schools can turn it off.
  - A planned change gives credit for a bot-assisted redo toward the "five correct answers in a row" needed to advance.
  - When the tutor gave hints instead of answers, "most students largely stopped using it."

  — [FutureEd](https://www.future-ed.org/students-tried-khanmigos-ai-tutor-most-didnt-want-its-help/)
- **Khan's own A/B tests** (blog, May 2026; about 20 tests, Oct 2025-Apr 2026, more than 15 million tutoring threads):
  - A background **"math agent" verifies calculations** in real time.
  - Limiting the agent to the math the student had already done (instead of working out remaining steps) "reduced giving away the answer by 50%."
  - Giving the tutor a summary of recent attempts raised next-item correctness by +3.4% (608K threads).
  - Surfacing unmastered prerequisites with a brief review raised it by +2.7% (1.36M threads).
  - The previous 24 hours of conversation log, as plain text, raised "cognitive engagement" by 5.09%. JSON-format logs showed no gain.
  - Showing example problems had no effect.
  - Guardrail metrics are "answers given away before a student responds," math error rate and interactions per thread. A change ships only if its chance to win is above 0.95 with no guardrail harm.

  — [Khan Academy Blog: Building a better AI tutor](https://blog.khanacademy.org/how-khan-academy-is-building-a-better-ai-tutor-our-most-recent-learnings/)
- Earlier Khan finding: Khanmigo's math accuracy improves when it has the human-written exercise, steps, hints and solutions before it calculates or evaluates. — [Khan Academy Blog: Math computation and tutoring updates](https://blog.khanacademy.org/khanmigo-math-computation-and-tutoring-updates/)

**Brilliant Koji**
- Koji focuses on "the relevant step" and asks "a guiding question," explaining another way "without turning the lesson into an answer lookup." The App Store copy says Brilliant "doesn't give you answers." — [Brilliant](https://brilliant.org/resources/choosing-brilliant/is-brilliant-worth-it/); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/brilliant-learn-by-doing/id913335252)
- One aggregated Reddit-sourced complaint (before Koji) said learning is "one-way," with no way to get clarity. — [Brighterly](https://brighterly.com/blog/is-brilliant-org-worth-it)

**Independent AI-tutor evaluations**
- **Bastani et al., PNAS 2025.** Field experiment with nearly 1,000 Turkish high-school students (grades 9-11) using GPT-4.
  - "GPT Base", a ChatGPT-like interface, improved practice grades by 48%, but exam performance fell 17% once access was removed (preprint figure).
  - "GPT Tutor", with learning safeguards, improved practice by 127%, and exam scores came out about equal to the control group.
  - Students overestimated how much they had learned.

  — [Wharton Knowledge](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/without-guardrails-generative-ai-can-harm-education); [SSRN preprint](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4895486); [PNAS record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nas/journl/v122y2025pe2422633122.html)
- **Google LearnLM and Eedi, arXiv Dec 2025.** Exploratory RCT with 165 students in five UK secondary schools.
  - LearnLM drafted each tutoring message, and an expert tutor approved, edited or rewrote it. **76.4% were approved with zero or minimal edits.**
  - LearnLM students did "at least as well" as human-tutored students, and were 5.5 pp more likely to solve novel problems on later topics (66.2% vs 60.7%).
  - Tutors said LearnLM was strong at drafting Socratic questions.

  — [arXiv 2512.23633](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23633v1); [Stanford SCALE note](https://scale.stanford.edu/news/research-notes-two-emerging-strategies-using-ai-tutoring)
- Eedi's separate 20-school trial found no clear difference at six months and an effect size of 0.17 at twelve months. A larger four-arm trial runs to July 2026. — [Eedi Substack](https://eedi.substack.com/p/ai-in-education-7-five-things-i-learned)
- **ChatGPT Study Mode and Gemini Guided Learning:** hands-on reviews only, and they are mixed. One tester found Gemini "often blurts out answers too quickly and sometimes fabricates user responses." Others found ChatGPT "too eager to rush ahead." — [Yahoo/Mashable test](https://tech.yahoo.com/ai/articles/put-chatgpt-gemini-study-modes-123000726.html); [Why Try AI](https://www.whytryai.com/p/ai-study-modes)
- Other platforms report that "known human support raises engagement with AI tutoring where availability alone does not" (Robinson et al., 2026, as cited in the Khanmigo RCT). — [EdWorkingPaper 26-1551](https://edworkingpapers.com/sites/default/files/ai26-1551.pdf)

### Inferences
- The Khanmigo data favor an **AI tutor that is triggered, not just made available**. It should open on the wrong-answer screen with one concrete Socratic question about the student's own step, and should not wait to be summoned through a chat icon. It also suggests replacing free chat with **suggested-reply chips** and structured inputs, since 24% of real messages were already prompt clicks and 39% were bare numbers.
- Offering **credit for a tutor-assisted redo** (Khan's planned change) is a concrete way to make engaging with the tutor worthwhile when mastery is gated.
- Guardrail architecture worth copying:
  - a separate deterministic **math verifier** (CAS) that checks every number the LLM states;
  - give the tutor the human-written solution and hints for the item;
  - restrict it to the steps the student has already done;
  - measure "answer given away before the student responded" as a release-blocking metric.
- For photo exam checking, the Google-Eedi "AI drafts, human approves" model and Snorkl's teacher-approval toggle are direct precedents for a teacher-in-the-loop mode.
- Pedagogically, a hint ladder with a cap (DreamBox), prior-grade Boosts (Zearn) and a full worked explanation after a miss (IXL) can coexist. The order can be: try again, then a targeted hint, then an AI question, then a worked solution plus a similar retry item.

### Gaps
- I found no independent published measurement of Khanmigo's math error rate. Khan reports only that accuracy "held steady" across model changes.
- I could not confirm from primary sources how many hints Khan Academy exercises offer, or whether hints are available before the first attempt on mobile. A user thread said hints appeared only after a wrong answer, but it is undated ([Khan support community](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/bg/community/posts/360072222631-Hint-Option-is-Gone)).
- Smartick's and Matific's wrong-answer flows were not documented in sources I could reach. A search summary said one reviewer describes Smartick lessons as "question-based, with guidance that steers the child toward the correct method," but I could not pin it to a specific page (candidate: [Smarter Learning Guide](https://smarterlearningguide.com/smartick-math-review)). Treat it as unverified.

## 5. Motivation: points, streaks, badges, avatars, leagues, mascots and progress maps, and which help or hurt at this age

### Takeaway
Every app uses some extrinsic layer. Brilliant runs Duolingo-style XP, streaks and weekly 30-person leagues. DreamBox uses a weekly streak tied to five lessons. Smartick has "Ticks" spent in a virtual world with games unlocked after the session. Zearn uses the Tower of Power as a visible goal. IXL relies on the score itself. Meta-analyses show modest positive effects of gamification on motivation in math (g of about 0.38). Leaderboards mainly build extrinsic motivation, and the clearest documented harm for this age group is score-loss anxiety (IXL).

### Cited Findings
- **Brilliant:** free and Premium users "track progress with streaks and XP and compete in Leagues." — [Brilliant pricing help](https://brilliant.org/help/pricing-and-plans)
- **Brilliant leagues:** 30 players per league, reset every Monday at 3 AM UTC, with promotion and demotion by XP rank. There are ten tiers named after elements (Hydrogen up to Einsteinium). — [Wikipedia: Brilliant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brilliant_(website))
- One Brilliant App Store reviewer (April 2025) complained it "doesn't really game it up enough" compared with Duolingo and is "way more expensive." Another mentions "streak rechargers" and "ranks." — [App Store: Brilliant reviews](https://apps.apple.com/app/brilliant-learn-by-doing/id913335252)
- **DreamBox 6-8:** students self-monitor from a tab on the Lesson Chooser that shows weekly lessons completed and a **Week Streak** (consecutive weeks with at least 5 lessons). These feed a Stats page under "My Stuff". The environment is "gamified" with "engagement areas." — [DreamBox 6-8 guide](https://info.discoveryeducation.com/rs/063-SDC-839/images/TeacherGuide-6-8-Math.pdf)
- **Smartick:** students earn "Ticks" (virtual points) that work "like money in the Smartick world." Rewards populate a virtual world and unlock games after the daily 15 minutes. — [Getting Smart](https://www.gettingsmart.com/2016/11/smart-review-daily-math-app-simple-approach-proven-results/); [Homeschool review](https://teachablescottstotshomeschool.blogspot.com/2019/10/homeschool-review-crew-smartick.html) (older, 2016-2019); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/smartick/id823586852)
- **Matific:** consumer listings mention virtual rewards and trophies. This comes from a search summary of the store listing, not a full read. — [App Store: Matific (via search excerpt)](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1440019986)
- **IXL:** IXL frames the SmartScore as building "productive struggle" and a "magical feeling of earned achievement" at 100. — [IXL SmartScore Guide](https://www.ixl.com/materials/SmartScore_Guide.pdf)
- Student reviews of IXL describe the opposite. One says "smartscore of 79 almost done... Then get one wrong and im back down to 57." Another: "it just makes me overthink and makes me cry myself to sleep." Others say "kids in my class have cried" and that it takes "HOURS just to receive an A." These are self-selected Trustpilot reviews. — [Trustpilot IXL](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/ixl.com?page=10); [Trustpilot UK](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/ixl.com?page=5); a petition exists to "Change IXL's SmartScore rate" ([change.org](https://www.change.org/p/paul-mishkin-change-ixl-s-smartscore-rate))
- **Research:**
  - A 2026 *Educational Psychology Review* systematic review and meta-analysis on math (secondary and higher education, 11 studies) found a small-to-moderate positive effect of gamification on motivation (g = 0.383, 95% CI 0.11-0.66). Only four studies in the wider review reported negative effects, and the authors call the evidence mixed. — [Springer, Ed Psych Review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-025-10108-1)
  - A 2020 meta-analysis (N = 3,083) found g = 0.464 for gamification on learning outcomes and examined 14 design elements, including leaderboards. — [Springer ETR&D](https://springerlink.bibliotecabuap.elogim.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s11423-020-09807-z.pdf)
  - Leaderboard-centered social gamification built extrinsic more than intrinsic motivation (Finnish study, via a scoping review). A middle-school math study found the effect depends on player type: social leaderboards suited social players, and badges suited achievement-oriented players. — [Scoping review: gamification and motivation in math](https://paperity.org/p/371453110/the-role-of-gamification-in-increasing-motivation-to-learn-mathematics-a-scoping-review)

### Inferences
- For grades 7-8, the safer core is a **streak counted in days or weeks of completing a short lesson** (DreamBox's Week Streak is gentler than a daily streak), **XP for effort and correct retries**, and a **visible course map**. Leagues should be optional or class-scoped, because the evidence says they mostly build extrinsic motivation.
- Avoid a mastery number that visibly **drops** by large amounts after one error. IXL's design produces documented distress. If a score must decay, use Khan-style level labels or Zearn-style "complete with help" instead.
- Rewarding **use of the tutor after an error** (XP or redo credit) addresses the main engagement problem found in the Khanmigo RCT.

### Gaps
- I found no rigorous study isolating the effect of streaks or leagues specifically on 12-14-year-olds in math apps.
- I could not verify current (2025-2026) DreamBox 6-8 or Zearn MS reward mechanics beyond the 2022 guide and help excerpts. Zearn badges are not documented in the sources I reached.

## 6. Teacher side: dashboards, assignments, and what teachers see

### Takeaway
The teacher products share three features: **alerts on repeated struggle** (Zearn Tower Alerts, DreamBox alerts), **usage and pacing against a weekly goal** (DreamBox five lessons a week, Zearn Pace/Progress), and **teacher-assigned content alongside the adaptive path** (DreamBox blue-icon assignments, Khan placement overrides). AI is now entering teacher tools (Khanmigo teacher tools free in the US, Matific's AI Teacher Assistant), and teacher-approval gates on AI feedback are appearing (Snorkl).

### Cited Findings
- **Zearn:** Tower Alerts fire when a student gets 3 Boosts in one Tower of Power. Zearn recommends that teachers check the Pace, Progress, Sprint and Tower Alert reports weekly. A LiveView report and a School Goals report also exist. Tutors use Tower Alerts to form small groups. — [Zearn help: Tower Alerts (via search excerpt)](https://help.zearn.org/hc/en-us/articles/236239748); [Zearn reporting suite](https://help.zearn.org/hc/en-us/articles/29008224450967-Zearn-reporting-suite); [LiveView Report](https://help.zearn.org/hc/en-us/articles/41582158077975-LiveView-Report); [TN Zearn one-pager](https://tn.gov/content/dam/tn/education/tn-all-corps/REVISED_Zearn_One-Pager_FY23pdf.pdf)
- **Zearn:** daily digital assessments "check for understanding and notify teachers when students are struggling". There is also a paper Exit Ticket for each lesson, less scaffolded than the Tower. — [Zearn MS page](https://about.zearn.org/middle-school-math); [Zearn help: lesson-level assessments (via search excerpt)](https://help.zearn.org/hc/en-us/articles/115007900828)
- **DreamBox Insights Dashboard (6-8, 2022):**
  - The Home page has a Total Lessons column with a classroom filter and lets teachers send messages to students about progress.
  - Reports include monthly usage, Student Usage and an Activity Feed of lesson cards.
  - A "Play Lesson" button lets the teacher replay a student's lesson in a demo environment, so the student's data is not disturbed.
  - Assignments show on the student's Lesson Chooser with a blue icon (short- or long-term).
  - Students can send a lesson screenshot to the teacher.
  - A Family Dashboard emails guardians about learning gains.
  - The product is available in English and Spanish.

  — [DreamBox 6-8 guide](https://info.discoveryeducation.com/rs/063-SDC-839/images/TeacherGuide-6-8-Math.pdf)
- **Khan Academy:** teachers monitor progress through the platform dashboard and can review and adjust MAP-based placements. Khan facilitated rostering and dashboard monitoring in the district study. — [EdWorkingPaper 26-1551](https://edworkingpapers.com/sites/default/files/ai26-1551.pdf)
- **Khanmigo teacher tools** (free for US teachers) reportedly include lesson planning, rubric generation, quiz questions, exit tickets, hooks and student progress summaries. Schools can disable the student tutor. — [Neowin](https://www.neowin.net/news/khan-academys-khanmigo-ai-tool-to-be-free-for-us-educators-thanks-to-microsoft/); [aitoolsatlas (secondary)](https://aitoolsatlas.ai/tools/khanmigo/pricing); [FutureEd](https://www.future-ed.org/students-tried-khanmigos-ai-tutor-most-didnt-want-its-help/)
- **IXL:** recommends a goal of SmartScore 80 in at least two skills per week, which gives teachers a weekly target. — [IXL SmartScore Guide](https://www.ixl.com/materials/SmartScore_Guide.pdf)
- **Brilliant:** teachers can search by topic, grade or standard, and a progress dashboard shows lessons completed and time spent. — [Tech & Learning](https://www.techlearning.com/learning/stem/what-is-brilliant-and-how-can-teachers-use-it)
- **Matific:** a review calls the dashboard (Activities, Reports, Class Management) its most impressive feature. A 2025 help article describes an "AI Teacher Assistant" that answers plain-language questions from class data. — [MindShare Learning](https://mindsharelearning.ca/?p=13582); [Matific Intercom help](https://intercom.help/matific-f4fbfc9df4d1/en-us/articles/15485869)
- **Snorkl:** teachers choose auto-release or teacher approval of AI feedback and can review individual responses with the AI feedback attached. — [Ditch That Textbook](https://ditchthattextbook.com/snorkl/)

### Inferences
- A minimum teacher dashboard for the Hebrew app could include:
  - a weekly-goal column (lessons or skills against target);
  - a struggle-alert feed generated by repeated errors or tutor escalations, Zearn-style;
  - a per-skill heatmap;
  - assignments that appear in the student's map with a distinct icon;
  - for photo exam checking, an **AI-drafted grade and comment queue the teacher approves or edits** (Snorkl and Google-Eedi pattern).
- DreamBox's "replay the student's lesson in demo mode" and the student-to-teacher "send screenshot" button are cheap, high-value features that fit a phone app well.

### Gaps
- I found no 2025-2026 documentation of IXL Analytics screens (e.g., "Trouble spots") or of the Khan Academy teacher dashboard's exact contents.
- I found no data on how often teachers act on alerts.

## 7. Mobile UI patterns worth copying and common complaints in reviews

### Takeaway
The patterns worth copying are: one short daily lesson with a visible completion goal (DreamBox, Smartick, Zearn), help controls placed next to the answer and next buttons (Khan's "Tutor Me"), suggested-prompt chips instead of an empty chat box, an AI that opens itself after an error and sees the problem state (Khan 2026, Brilliant Koji), and a custom math keypad. Common complaints are punitive score drops and anxiety (IXL), equivalent answers graded wrong, too much repetitive volume, high price and billing problems, weak review tools (Brilliant), and web-only features on mobile (Khan).

### Cited Findings
- **Khan placement of help:** in the study period, help was a "prominent 'Tutor Me' control at the lower right of the screen, beside the buttons for advancing to the next question." Even so, use stayed low until Khanmigo was switched to open by default (29 Jan 2026). — [EdWorkingPaper 26-1551](https://edworkingpapers.com/sites/default/files/ai26-1551.pdf)
- **Suggested prompt chips** ("Help me solve this. Ask me one question at a time.", "Help me check my answer.") made up 24.2% of student messages. — [EdWorkingPaper 26-1551](https://edworkingpapers.com/sites/default/files/ai26-1551.pdf)
- **Brilliant Koji** "starts from the lesson's state rather than a blank prompt box" (secondary). Brilliant itself says Koji "can see the lesson and problem a learner is working on." — [chatgate.ai on Koji (secondary)](https://chatgate.ai/post/koji-by-brilliant); [Brilliant](https://brilliant.org/resources/choosing-brilliant/is-brilliant-worth-it/)
- **DreamBox Lesson Chooser:** student choice plus assigned lessons, with a right-side tab showing weekly progress and streak. Recommended practice is "one assigned lesson, then one choice lesson." — [DreamBox 6-8 guide](https://info.discoveryeducation.com/rs/063-SDC-839/images/TeacherGuide-6-8-Math.pdf)
- **Smartick:** the session comes first and play afterwards, so games unlock only after the 15-minute session. — [App Store: Smartick](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/smartick/id823586852)
- **Brilliant free-tier gating:** two lessons a day and sequential navigation. Premium unlocks "jump to any lesson." — [Brilliant](https://brilliant.org/resources/choosing-brilliant/is-brilliant-worth-it/)
- **Complaints, IXL:** sharp SmartScore drops, crying and anxiety, hours of repetitive questions, glitches, and equivalent answers marked wrong (Trustpilot, self-selected). — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/ixl.com?page=10); [Cal Poly blog (weak)](https://grandavehousing.calpoly.edu/blog/ixl-feedback-decoding-user-experiences-and-reviews-1764807139)
- **Complaints, Brilliant:**
  - The app "lacks the ability to easily review past information" and has removed courses (April 2025).
  - Emails are "really annoying".
  - It is expensive compared with Duolingo.
  - Some users report being charged annual fees after believing they had cancelled (2024-2025).
  - It has few settings.

  — [App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/app/brilliant-learn-by-doing/id913335252); [Brilliant review roundup (Edwize)](https://edwize.org/brilliant-review)
- **Complaints, Khan Academy app:** some functionality is web-only. An older (2020) review reported a wrong answer key ("6 × 3" expecting 45). — [App Store: Khan Academy](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/khan-academy/id469863705)
- **Complaints, Khanmigo:** in practice students tried to get the answer and stopped using the tutor when it gave hints instead. "Seeking help with one's own confusion remained a choice, and most students declined it most of the time." — [FutureEd](https://www.future-ed.org/students-tried-khanmigos-ai-tutor-most-didnt-want-its-help/)
- **Complaints, Smartick:** per-child subscription cost and mixed views on pricing. — [Trustpilot Smartick](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/smartick.com)

### Inferences
- Phone lesson screen pattern to adopt:
  - a progress bar or "tower" stage indicator at the top;
  - one problem per screen;
  - a custom keypad docked at the bottom;
  - a single primary "Check" button;
  - after an error, a feedback sheet sliding up with a short explanation, a "Try again" button and an **auto-opened tutor question with 2-3 reply chips**;
  - after a second error, a worked solution plus a similar retry item.

  For Hebrew, the screen should be RTL while math expressions stay LTR.
- To avoid the most common complaints:
  - keep grade-like numbers off the student screen;
  - accept equivalent forms;
  - cap the daily session length (10-15 minutes) instead of open-ended "until 100";
  - give students a "review past lessons" view, which Brilliant users miss;
  - avoid aggressive email and notification nagging.
- An app that gates games or social features until after the daily lesson (Smartick) keeps play from competing with practice.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve 2025-2026 App Store or Google Play review text for IXL, Zearn or DreamBox. Zearn and DreamBox are mainly used on school Chromebooks, iPads and the web rather than phones, and I found no evidence about their phone-specific UX.
- I found no published UX research (A/B tests) from these vendors on feedback-screen design. The only exception is Khan's May 2026 blog on tutor latency and context.
- I found no Hebrew-language or Israeli-market evidence for any of these products in this scope.
